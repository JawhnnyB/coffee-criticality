# The people on Unit 1

DRAFT, written by Claude on 24 November 2026, waiting for the owner's approval.

This is the reference every line of dialogue is written from. Each person has a history, something they want, something they are afraid of, a mistake they made that still shapes them, and a way of talking nobody else on the floor shares. If a line could be said by anyone in the cast, it is the wrong line.

Two rules for all of them:

- **They talk to you, not at you.** Nobody recites a rule. When someone teaches you something (LOTOTO, two sets of eyes, say the number first), they tell you why it matters to them, usually because of something that happened.
- **Thank-yous and apologies are specific.** "Thank you" always comes with what you did and what it saved. "I'm sorry" always comes with what they did. Nobody apologises for feelings in general.

Where a mistake is told, it is told once. The table at the end lists who tells what, and where, so no story is told twice.

---

## Dr. Mabel Quinn — the café

**Who she is.** About seventy. Forty years on this site, twenty of them running the floor. She retired two years ago and found she couldn't stay home, so she runs the café. The kettle came out of the old control room; she carried it out herself the day they gutted it. Her husband says she retired from the job, not the building.

**What she wants.** Someone to give the chalk to. The north wall has a mark for every catch said out loud, and she wants it kept honest after she's gone.

**What she's afraid of.** Becoming a nice old lady with a kettle that people are polite to. When she ran the floor, people were scared of her, and scared people hid things from her. She found out later.

**Her mistake.** Her first year, a tank level read high and she decided the gauge was wrong. A relief valve had stuck. She never told anyone, and she started the wall because of it. (Tier 2 scene.)

**How she talks.** Unhurried, teasing, asks questions back. Calls you "love" once you've sat with her. Tells small stories instead of making points. Notices who hasn't come in for coffee. Never raises her voice; when she's disappointed she gets quieter and kinder, which is worse.

**Her people.** She trained Elena. She's known Holt since he was twenty-four and is the only person alive who calls him Reggie. She saves Jordan the last pastry. Tommy comes in for his coffee before ten and doesn't smile until after.

## Mr. Reginald Holt — the director

**Who he is.** Mid-fifties. Started here as a junior engineer under Mabel. Runs the site now. Eats lunch at his desk. Stands up when people come in, because someone once told him he didn't.

**What he wants.** Bad news early, while it is cheap. A time, a place, and what you saw. He can act on that. He can't act on "concerning".

**What he's afraid of.** Being the last person to know. He knows people tell him what they think he wants to hear, and it makes him lonely and expensive to talk to.

**His mistakes.** As deputy, he signed the review-budget cut with the director before him; six months later a pump ran with a tag still on it (tier 2). Early on he tidied a log so a shift would look cleaner (crew moment "Read twice"). On his first review board he voted with his boss because he wanted the boss's job (tier 3).

**How he talks.** Complete sentences, few contractions, no adjectives. Dry, occasionally very funny without smiling. Precise thanks: he thanks you for the specific twenty minutes. When he admits something, he does it plainly and moves on, which is how you know it cost him.

**His people.** Asks Elena's opinion before anyone else's and neither of them mentions it. Lets Priya talk him into walkdown hours, slowly. Knows Mabel is half right about him.

## Elena Voss — day shift supervisor

**Who she is.** Late thirties. Supervisor for nine years. Her daughter is six; Elena drops her at her sister's at five every morning, so she's always first in. The child seat stays in the van. It reminds her who everybody on the floor is working for.

**What she wants.** Nobody alone with a problem. That is what "two sets of eyes" means to her: not checking you, two people checking the plant.

**What she's afraid of.** A handover where something slips while she's tired. She reads the night log in her van with a flashlight before relieving anyone, then walks what it says.

**Her mistakes.** She transposed two valve numbers on a lineup card, and you catch it (tier 3). Years ago she moved a clearance tag to save twenty minutes (crew moment "The tag on 2B"). On her first nights, the board told her about a stopped pump twenty minutes after a camera did (crew moment "I asked for you").

**How she talks.** Calm, practical, short. Asks if you've eaten. Gives instructions, then softens them with the reason. Admits when she's tired. When something moves her she gets quieter, not louder. Never says anything about you that she wouldn't say to your face.

**Her people.** Mabel trained her; they still argue about how strong coffee should be. She gave Jordan his job when no one else would look at him. Holt asks her first.

## Tommy Reyes — maintenance

**Who he is.** About fifty. Twenty-eight years turning wrenches. Talks to pumps. Has a kid who gave him a keychain with a little plastic wrench on it; he looks at it every time he hangs a lock. Bad knees when rain is coming.

**What he wants.** His crew home with all ten fingers. Engineers who come down to the shop instead of signing from a chair.

**What he's afraid of.** Being the reason someone gets hurt. It nearly happened twice.

**His mistakes.** His second year, he hid a weeping flange under a rag and a friend took the spray (crew moment "The drip tray"). Ten years ago he skipped nothing, pushed the try button, and the pump turned over: a second breaker not on the drawing, with his apprentice's hand on the coupling (tier 2). The first story is why he never hides anything; the second is why he never skips the try.

**How he talks.** Plain, funny, self-deprecating. Calls engineers "boss" and doesn't mean it. Short with you when he's worried, then apologises for it: he's not mad at you, he's mad at the card. LOTOTO is "the four words", and he'll make you say them.

**His people.** Once sent back a work package Marcus had signed; they've been careful with each other since. Doesn't smile before ten unless something has gone right. Says he isn't coming to bowling, then always comes.

## Marcus Hale — senior engineer

**Who he is.** Mid-thirties. The youngest senior engineer the site has had, and he mentions it first now because everyone else does. Brilliant, quick, prickly. Hardest on himself.

**What he wants.** To be right. Underneath that, to stop being afraid of being wrong.

**What he's afraid of.** Being wrong in a room. His first year as senior he put the bad number on slide nine of a meeting, and Holt found it. Holt didn't shout. He asked why it was on slide nine. Marcus still doesn't have an answer (tier 1).

**His mistakes.** Monday's heat balance: the feedwater flow was off by a percent and a bit, and he told you before the floor (tier 2), then the board (tier 3). On day 3 the dose sticker came from his own survey sheet, which is why he wants it to be a typo. Before the power ascension he asks you to check his boron sheet (crew moment "Check my sheet").

**How he talks.** Fast, exact, sarcastic when cornered. Tests people, then apologises for testing them. Apologises awkwardly and often, and means it every time. His teaching is one line he learned the hard way: say the number before you defend it.

**His people.** Priya started the same year; she is the only one who can tease him. Tommy sent back his work package once. He snapped at Jordan over the sticker, then apologised twice.

## Dr. Priya Sharma — reactor engineering

**Who she is.** Early forties. Core physics. Scuffed boots, spotless desk. Her shoes have more miles on them than her car. Cried in a stairwell on her first day and will tell you so if you look overwhelmed.

**What she wants.** For people to feel the physics as real heat in real metal. To walk everything she signs.

**What she's afraid of.** Becoming someone who knows the model better than the plant.

**Her mistakes.** Her first walkdown, the drawing put a valve on the wrong side of a pipe (tier 1). Her first reload, she put the fresh fuel together in the middle because it looked tidy (tier 2). Once she waved off a high reading because the map said it couldn't be (crew moment "Next to the wall").

**How she talks.** Warm, precise, a teacher. Gets visibly excited about neutrons. Asks what you think before telling you. Says "teaching model, not a license" as something she believes, not a disclaimer: we learn from it, we don't trust it blindly.

**Her people.** Protective of Marcus. Pushes Holt for walkdown hours. Thinks watching Tommy hang a lockout is like watching someone play an instrument.

---

## Jordan Peck — floor tech

**Who he is.** Late twenties. Floor tech by day, night rover on the watch rotation. Bowling captain. Finder of the good coffee. The social glue of the floor.

**What he wants.** A crew where people say things out loud, in the break room, where the door can hear.

**What he's afraid of.** Being the name on the log again.

**His mistake.** At his last plant, someone hid a bad reading on nights, and Jordan's name was on the log for that hour. He had signed it at shift end without reading it. Everybody did. Nobody believed he didn't know, and he says he wouldn't have believed himself either. He left (tier 2). That is the distance: not judging anyone, just never signing blind again.

**How he talks.** Chatty, funny, contractions everywhere, "honestly". When he's hurt he doesn't get angry; he gets polite and quiet, which lands harder. On the radio at night he narrates the rounds like a friend keeping you company; if the week went badly, it's one or two words a space and "writing my own log tonight".

**His people.** Elena gave him this job; he'd walk through a wall for her. Mabel saves him the last pastry and he pretends not to know.
